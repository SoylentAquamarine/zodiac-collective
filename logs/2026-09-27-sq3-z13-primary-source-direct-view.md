# Direct view of the Z13 primary source: the repeat-pattern claim is now confirmed at the strongest available tier

**Trigger:** the Z13 repeat-pattern open question (`knowledge-base/state.md`) has repeatedly named "this
project's own direct view of the primary 1970 scan" as the remaining gate before the pattern (position
equalities 1=12, 3=11, 5=7=9, 8=13) could be treated as settled, rather than resting on a directly-fetched
but still secondary source (Wikisource's transcription page). This log is that direct view.

## Method (disclosed in full)

Located the actual cipher glyph image via the Wikimedia Commons file used to illustrate the Wikisource
transcription of the 1970 letter (`File:Zodiac-name-cipher.png`), confirmed via the Wikimedia API as a
genuine, dated, public-domain, provenance-attributed extraction from the original letter scan (full
provenance: `data/external-sources/wikimedia-z13-name-cipher-2026-09-27/README.source.md`). Downloaded the
actual PNG directly (not through an AI summarization tool), verified it as a genuine 450×28 image, and
viewed it directly — first at full size, then via local, tool-free pixel upscaling (nearest/Lanczos
resize, no OCR, no AI transcription) of the whole sequence and two closer crops (positions ~4–9 and ~9–13)
to check the more ambiguous hand-drawn symbols carefully before concluding.

## What was found

The 13-position sequence, transcribed by direct visual inspection: **A, E, N, [circled crosshair/target,
unique], [circled pinwheel/segmented glyph], K, [same pinwheel/segmented glyph again], M, [same
pinwheel/segmented glyph a third time], [hook/shepherd's-crook shape, unique], N, A, M.**

On first, lower-magnification viewing, position 7's glyph looked visually distinct from positions 5 and 9
(a plausible reading error, disclosed here rather than silently corrected away) — a closer, heavily
upscaled crop of positions 4 through 9 resolved this: positions 5, 7, and 9 show the same internal
segmented/pinwheel pattern, consistent with each other and distinct from position 4's crosshair symbol.
This first-impression discrepancy and its resolution are recorded here explicitly, since getting this
kind of primary-source read right the first time isn't guaranteed, and disclosing the correction process
is more honest than presenting only the final, confident answer.

## Result: independent confirmation of the standing repeat-pattern claim

This directly confirms, from the primary image itself rather than any secondary transcription: **position
1 = position 12 (`A`=`A`); position 3 = position 11 (`N`=`N`); positions 5, 7, and 9 all share one
repeated glyph; position 8 = position 13 (`M`=`M`).** This is an exact match to the pattern already on
record in `knowledge-base/state.md` (1=12, 3=11, 5=7=9, 8=13), previously sourced to a directly-fetched
Wikisource page — one tier below a direct image view. **This closes the "primary-scan-direct-view gate"**
that entry named as the remaining step before this specific structural claim could be treated as fully
settled, independent of any secondary source's own transcription accuracy.

## What this does and does not settle

This settles the **structural repeat-pattern claim** (which positions share a symbol) at the strongest
tier this project can reach without institutional archive access. It does **not** solve Z13, identify what
the repeated glyph or any other symbol represents, or say anything about who sent the letter — this
analysis is a glyph-position comparison only, and no suspect-related content was read, summarized, or
cited anywhere in this process, consistent with this project's absolute ethical boundary.

**Separately, still unconfirmed**: the "repeated eight-ball symbol acting as a separator" claim (a
distinct, previously-unverified claim named in `knowledge-base/state.md`, not addressed by this check) —
whether any of the glyphs observed here corresponds to what other sources call an "eight-ball" symbol, and
whether it functions as a structural separator, was not evaluated in this pass and remains open.
