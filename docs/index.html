This project studies a cipher, not a suspect.

Our objective is the cryptanalysis of Z13 as a text/cipher problem — never the identification of a suspect. We document publicly established case history and previously published claims, but we do not name new suspects, endorse an identification, or publish new accusatory claims about any living or identifiable private individual. Wins so far · in plain English

What we have actually accomplished

We have measured why a unique Z13 solution is so difficult and verified its repeated-symbol pattern. This does not identify a suspect or produce a solution.

Calibration status: the collision-compatible rerun strongly separates true English in six ordinary Z408 windows. However, those windows have 12–13 distinct cipher classes; exhaustive checking finds no 13-character Z408 window with Z13's eight-class repeat structure. The result shows that some 13-character homophonic windows are scoreable, but it does not yet close Z13's structure-specific sufficiency question.

01

Thirteen symbols are far too few for a unique key

A reproducible calculation estimates roughly 59 characters to pin down a full Z408-like 54-symbol homophonic key, but that is not Z13's observed problem: Z13 contains only eight distinct symbol classes. Their unrestricted assignments carry about 37.6 bits, which yields an average unicity-distance heuristic near 11.8 characters if one assumes 3.2 bits of ordinary-English redundancy per character. That arithmetic correction does not make Z13 solved or uniquely solvable—the redundancy estimate is asymptotic, Z13 may be a name or short phrase, and the result needs calibration on 13-character windows from solved ciphers. We have now inspected a checksummed crop of the actual 1970 cipher and independently confirmed that positions 1=12, 3=11, 5=7=9, and 8=13. That verifies the pattern, not a plaintext: many plausible readings can still fit such a short cipher.

02

A proposed safeguard against overfitting failed its first audit

Z13's repeat pattern contains eight distinct ciphertext classes. Limiting each plaintext letter to seven homophones sounds strict, but across the 26-letter alphabet it excludes only the 26 constant mappings from 268 possibilities. It retains 208,827,064,550 mappings — 99.9999999875% of the unrestricted space. We therefore rejected this restriction before scoring candidates; any replacement must demonstrate real selectivity on short windows from solved ciphers. Claude's first replacement is also not ready to run: it does not define how 54 Z408 slots become eight Z13 slots or how tied ranks are handled. Standard largest-remainder scaling produces eight singleton slots—no homophony—so the rule must be specified before its search space can be counted. A subsequent count-based proposal also failed: allowing at most four colliding classes retains 200,465,865,600 mappings, or 95.9961% of the unrestricted space. A minimum useful reduction must now be defined before another model is proposed.

Why Z13 is a different problem than Z408 and Z340

The two other Zodiac ciphers were solved. This one is 13 symbols long.

Z408 (408 symbols) was solved in about a week in 1969. Z340 (340 symbols) took 51 years but was solved in 2020 and confirmed by the FBI. Z13 is far shorter than either — short enough that this project's first real task is formally establishing whether a unique solution is even possible without an outside clue, not assuming it is because the other two were solved.
